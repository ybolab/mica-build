# PLAN-078 Short-lived signer certificates: expiry the device enforces alone

- **status**: completed
- **createdAt**: 2026-09-04 20:45
- **relatedTask**: RFCT-308
- **relatedPlans**: [PLAN-077](PLAN-077.md) (Gate A, the anchor and the rotation decision), [PLAN-070](PLAN-070.md) (the `meta/` seam and the two anchors), [PLAN-071](PLAN-071.md) (the freshness bound and the clock it rests on)

## Context

### What this record is for, and what the request approved

`docs/design/security-lifecycle.md` §1.2 records one gap in its own words:

> **Revocation** — a compromised signer is reissued and out-waited; there is
> no CRL path to devices.

*Out-waited* means the stolen signer stays valid to every device until its
certificate expires on its own — and today that certificate is minted with
`-days 730` (`docs/design/release-signing.md` §2.1). Two years is the
attacker's window.

The user asked for short-lived signer certificates as the answer, and stated
the recommendation — the signer should be short-lived. **That request is the
approval for designing this, and for nothing else.** This record ends at an
explicit approval boundary, as PLAN-070/071/072/076/077 do; the design below is
not approved by the request that produced it.

### What already exists — measured, and one correction to the brief

Two of the three levels are already in the tree, and the brief's account of
them is accurate. Verified against `main` at `5dede7f8`:

- `meta/rauc/` holds `ca.cert.pem`, `ca.key.pem`, `signer.cert.pem`,
  `signer.key.pem` (present in `/srv/mos/meta/rauc/`, generated by
  `pkgs/rauc/gen-dev-keys.sh`; the directory is gitignored, so a fresh worktree
  has none).
- The CA issues the signer; bundles are signed by the signer; the device's
  keyring is the **CA** certificate. `rootfs/build.sh` stages
  `meta/rauc/ca.cert.pem` to `/etc/rauc/keyring.pem`, and
  `packed-keyring-from-meta` (`verify/src/checks-root.ts`) asserts the baked
  keyring is byte-equal to it.
- **Rotating the signer therefore requires no anchor change.** What
  `release-signing.md` §2.4's overlap procedure rotates is the **CA**, and that
  is a different problem this plan does not solve (§7).
- `pkgs/rauc/gen-dev-keys.sh` mints the CA with
  `keyUsage=critical,keyCertSign,cRLSign`; the production ceremony
  (`release-signing.md` §2.1) sets the same. The capability to sign revocation
  lists was provisioned and has never been used.

**One correction, and it matters for §2.** The brief says an expired signer
would collide with "a factory reflash to a known-good older release". It does
not. `docs/design/release-artifacts.md` §1 gives a release two flashable
things: `<board>-mos-<epoch>.img` (role `image`) and
`mos-<board>-<epoch>.raucb` (role `bundle`). **A reflash writes the `.img`,
which carries no CMS signature and which RAUC never verifies.** Signer expiry
touches the `.raucb` path only. The collision is real but narrower than stated,
and §2 prices the part that is real.

**A second correction, smaller.** The signer's validity is not a new idea being
introduced here — the production ceremony already justifies a *short* signer
("Short validity (2 years) because reissuing a signer is cheap — it needs the
CA key, not a fleet update"). This plan does not invent the mechanism. It
shortens the number, and then pays for what shortening it actually costs, which
the existing sentence does not.

## The measurement — the load-bearing fact, taken rather than reasoned about

Everything below turns on one question the brief poses correctly: **does an
expired signer make previously published bundles uninstallable?** CMS can be
evaluated two ways — was the certificate valid *at signing time* (the CMS
carries a `signingTime` attribute), or is it valid *now* — and the two answers
have opposite consequences. This was measured, not assumed.

### How it was run

The pinned Debian trixie container
(`build-env/images.env` `IMAGE_DEBIAN_TRIXIE` =
`debian:trixie-slim@sha256:d7e12182ce18b85b93007c1dedf31f2d29e01ccf3182cc4017c709b6259bc132`)
with the distribution's **rauc 1.13** — the same upstream release
`pkgs/rauc/versions.env` pins — and OpenSSL 3.5.7. This is the tree's own
precedent for host-side trust measurement
(`tests/rauc-trust-negative-test.sh`, which records that reading and verifying
with a distro rauc is explicitly fair). Certificate windows and verification
clocks were moved with `libfaketime`; nothing was installed on any device.

Fixtures: a CA of the §2.1 shape, and signers differing only in validity —
`short` valid `[now-1d, now+2d]`, `stale` valid `[now-400d, now-370d]`, a
reissued `next` valid 30 days, and a foreign CA's signer. A `format=verity`
bundle, the only format `pkgs/rauc/system.conf.in` accepts.

### M1–M6: the semantics, by `rauc info`

```
$ openssl x509 -in ca/short.cert.pem -noout -dates
notBefore=Sep  3 20:33:54 2026 GMT
notAfter=Sep  6 20:33:54 2026 GMT

# The bundle's CMS does carry a signingTime, so both readings are available:
$ openssl cms -cmsout -inform DER -in sig.der -print | grep -A2 signingTime
            object: signingTime (1.2.840.113549.1.9.5)
            set:
              UTCTIME:Sep  4 20:33:55 2026 GMT

# M1  control, at the real clock
$ rauc info --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem short.raucb
Certificate Chain:
 0 Subject: O = mos measurement, CN = short
   Not After:  Sep  6 20:33:54 2026 GMT
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)

# M2  the SAME bundle, clock moved past notAfter
$ faketime -f '+10d' rauc info --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem short.raucb
signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate has expired
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)

# M3  +10d, with [keyring] use-bundle-signing-time=true
$ faketime -f '+10d' rauc --conf sys-true.conf info short.raucb
Certificate Chain:
 0 Subject: O = mos measurement, CN = short
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)

# M4  +10d, with use-bundle-signing-time=false — the control for M3
signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate has expired
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)

# M6  clock BEHIND notBefore
$ faketime -f '-10d' rauc info --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem short.raucb
signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate is not yet valid
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)
```

### M11: the same verdicts through `rauc install`, not only `rauc info`

Each case in its own container with its own `rauc service` on a private system
bus and `--override-boot-slot=A`. (The first attempt did not isolate them: a
leftover service kept owning `de.pengutronix.rauc` and served the next case at
the previous clock, producing three identical answers. That result was
discarded.)

```
# M11.1  clock=now, default semantics
 10% Verifying signature
 20% Verifying signature done.
LastError: Failed mounting bundle: Failed to open /dev/dm-0: No such file or directory

# M11.2  clock=+10d, default semantics
 10% Verifying signature
 20% Verifying signature failed.
LastError: signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate has expired

# M11.3  clock=+10d, use-bundle-signing-time=true
 20% Verifying signature done.

# M11.4  clock=now, use-bundle-signing-time=true
 20% Verifying signature done.
```

The install refusal is at the `Verifying signature` step and nowhere later.
Cases that pass verification fail afterwards on `/dev/dm-0` — the container has
no device-mapper node for the verity payload, which is an environment limit and
not a property of the bundle; it is *after* the step this measurement is about,
and it is reported here rather than hidden.

### M10: signing-time semantics are not a milder expiry — they are no expiry

This is the finding that decides §1. Under `use-bundle-signing-time=true` the
device trusts a timestamp **the key holder chose**. A bundle signed by the
`stale` signer — expired 370 days ago — with the signing host's clock rolled
back into its old window:

```
$ openssl verify -CAfile ca/ca.cert.pem ca/stale.cert.pem
error 10 at 0 depth lookup: certificate has expired

$ sigtime forged.raucb
  CMS signingTime: Aug 15 20:37:19 2025 GMT      # inside a window that closed 370 days ago

# M10a  default semantics
signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate has expired
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)

# M10b  use-bundle-signing-time=true
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)
```

**An attacker holding an expired signer is accepted.** So signing-time
semantics do not merely "keep old bundles installable"; they make expiry
unenforceable against the party expiry exists to stop.

`M12`: the same setting still refuses a foreign CA
(`unable to get local issuer certificate`), so what it relaxes is expiry alone,
not trust.

### M0: the release side gets no warning it is about to publish a dead bundle

```
$ rauc bundle --cert ca/stale.cert.pem --key ca/stale.key.pem stage stale.raucb
Creating 'verity' format bundle
rauc-Message: No keyring given, skipping signature verification
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)          # a bundle signed by a 370-day-expired signer
```

But **with** a keyring, rauc warns:

```
$ rauc bundle --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem --cert ca/next.cert.pem --key ca/next.key.pem stage w4.raucb
rauc-Message: Keyring given, doing signature verification
rauc-WARNING **: Certificate 1 (/O=sig/CN=next) will expire in less than a month!
```

`build/src/tools/rauc.ts`'s `bundleArgs` pushes `--cert`, `--key`, the stage
directory and the output, and **no `--keyring`** — so the tree never sees this
warning. `rauc info --keyring` does *not* emit it (measured: W1, W2), so the
read-back is not a substitute. This is a cheap gate and it is in §9's backlog.

### R: the archive is repairable, and the repair was measured end to end

```
# R2   rauc resign refuses once the signer has expired: it verifies the
#      INCOMING signature against the same clock first.
$ faketime -f '+10d' rauc resign --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem \
      --cert ca/next.cert.pem --key ca/next.key.pem short.raucb resigned.raucb
signature verification failed: Verify error: certificate has expired
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)

# R7   the RELEASE HOST sets use-bundle-signing-time=true in its OWN config
#      and resigns with a signer reissued under the SAME, UNCHANGED CA:
$ faketime -f '+10d' rauc --conf release-host.conf resign \
      --cert ca/next.cert.pem --key ca/next.key.pem short.raucb resigned.raucb
Resigning 'verity' format bundle
rauc-WARNING **: Certificate 1 (/O=sig/CN=next) will expire in less than a month!
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)

# R8   and the result installs on an UNCHANGED device running default semantics
$ faketime -f '+10d' rauc info --keyring ca/ca.cert.pem resigned.raucb
 0 Subject: O = sig, CN = next
 1 Subject: O = mos measurement, CN = mos measurement CA
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)

# R9   the payload is byte-identical; only the CMS changed
original: f2f582c999c700d0c4dd27be0647f876e59a3a9d5d4f5c65c7fb87c120fd04f2  signature 2142 B
resigned: f2f582c999c700d0c4dd27be0647f876e59a3a9d5d4f5c65c7fb87c120fd04f2  signature 2141 B
PAYLOAD IDENTICAL -- only the CMS signature changed

# R10  and the forgery of M10b is still refused on the device
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)
```

`resign` needs loop and device-mapper access to check the verity payload under
exclusive access; the container was given them (`--privileged -v /dev:/dev`),
and host `/dev/mapper` and `losetup -a` were empty before and after.

### P: the third level is available only if the CA certificate says so

Both `gen-dev-keys.sh` and the §2.1 ceremony mint the CA with
`basicConstraints=critical,CA:TRUE,pathlen:0`. Two roots differing **only** in
that digit, each with an intermediate and a 35-day signer under it:

```
# P1  root pathlen:0, signer chained through an intermediate
$ rauc bundle --cert sig0.cert.pem --intermediate int0.cert.pem --key sig0.key.pem stage b0.raucb
$ rauc info --keyring root0.cert.pem b0.raucb
signature verification failed: Verify error: path length constraint exceeded
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)

# P2  root pathlen:1 — the only difference
 0 Subject: O = mos, CN = signer 1
 1 Subject: O = mos, CN = intermediate 1
 2 Subject: O = mos, CN = root pathlen 1
VERDICT: ACCEPTED (exit 0)

# P3  pathlen:1 but the intermediate not carried in the bundle
signature verification failed: Verify error: unable to get local issuer certificate
VERDICT: REFUSED (exit 1)
```

Also probed, on the rauc binary this tree builds
(`pkgs/rauc/out-amd64/rauc`): the `[keyring]` section understands
`check-crl`, `allow-partial-chain`, `use-bundle-signing-time` and
`check-purpose`. There is **no** RFC 3161 / trusted-timestamp support — the only
`timestamp` strings in the binary are slot-status fields. So there is no third
verification mode to reach for.

### What the measurement establishes

| # | Fact | Where |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | RAUC 1.13's default is **now** semantics: an expired signer makes every bundle it ever signed refuse, at `Verifying signature` | M2, M4, M11.2 |
| F2 | `[keyring] use-bundle-signing-time=true` switches to `signingTime` semantics | M3, M11.3 |
| F3 | The CMS carries a `signingTime`, set from the signing host's clock | M0 |
| F4 | `signingTime` is chosen by the key holder: an **expired** signer is accepted under F2 if the signing clock is rolled back | M10b |
| F5 | F2 relaxes expiry only; a foreign CA is still refused | M12 |
| F6 | A clock behind `notBefore` refuses with `certificate is not yet valid` | M6 |
| F7 | `rauc bundle` without `--keyring` signs with an expired signer silently; with `--keyring` it warns under a month | M0, W3, W4 |
| F8 | An archived bundle can be resigned under the **same CA** with a reissued signer, payload byte-identical, and installs on an unchanged device | R7–R9 |
| F9 | `resign` itself refuses at the device's semantics; the release host must opt into F2 **locally** to repair an archive | R2, R7 |
| F10 | An intermediate CA requires `pathlen:1` in the **root**, i.e. in the keyring; `pathlen:0` refuses | P1, P2 |
| F11 | The intermediate must be carried in the bundle (`--intermediate`), not only held by the issuer | P3 |
| F12 | rauc 1.13 has `check-crl`; it has no trusted-timestamp support | binary strings |

**The brief asked which behaviour the design needs, and whether RAUC can be
made to do it without patching it. The answer is: the behaviour it needs is
the one RAUC already does by default, and the tree already gets it — because
`pkgs/rauc/system.conf.in` never sets `use-bundle-signing-time`.** The
mechanism cost of this plan is zero. Every cost it carries is operational.

**And the brief's "if neither behaviour is acceptable, say so" is half-true and
worth stating precisely.** Neither is acceptable *as a device policy chosen in
isolation*: `now` breaks the archive, `signingTime` revokes nothing (F4). What
makes the design work is that **the two settings are not a fleet-wide choice
between two evils — they are two different machines' policies.** The device
takes `now`; the release host takes `signingTime` for the single operation of
repairing its own archive (F8, F9). That asymmetry is sound because the release
host is curated and the archive it repairs is its own, and it is measured
rather than hoped for.

## Proposal

### 1. The device verifies against **now**, and that is already what ships

No change to `pkgs/rauc/system.conf.in`. The decision this plan takes is to
make the absence of `use-bundle-signing-time` **deliberate and asserted**
rather than incidental: a verifier check that the rendered
`/etc/rauc/system.conf` does not set it, on the same footing as
`rauc-keyring-path`. Today the setting is absent because nobody added it, and
F4 makes it the single line that would silently disable everything below.

`check-purpose` and `allow-partial-chain` stay unset, unchanged from today.

### 2. The archive, and the obligation it creates

F1's cost is real and this is the whole of it: **`rauc install` of an archived
`.raucb` whose signer has expired is refused.** Three populations, priced
separately:

- **A factory reflash to a known-good older release — unaffected.** It writes
  the release's `.img`, which RAUC never verifies (Context). The recovery paths
  of `docs/design/recovery.md` that matter here are reflash paths; §7's "what
  no software path recovers" is untouched by this plan.
- **The release side's own archive — repairable, and it becomes an
  obligation.** F8: resign with a signer reissued under the unchanged CA,
  payload byte-identical. The obligation is that re-signing changes the
  bundle's bytes and therefore its `sha256`, which
  `release-artifacts.md` §1 pins in `SHA256SUMS` and §3 records in
  `manifest.json`'s `artifacts[]`. **A resigned bundle is a new release
  directory record, not an in-place edit**, and the publication gate must see
  it as one. This plan does not design that record; it names the coupling so
  the backlog item is not discovered late.
- **A customer holding a downloaded `.raucb` — not repairable by them.** They
  must re-download. This is the population that genuinely loses something, and
  it is the honest cost of the design. It is bounded: what they lose is a stale
  local copy, not their device, and the current release is always installable.

**What is deliberately not proposed: re-signing the whole archive on a
schedule.** That would restore "old bundles work forever" and with it the
property expiry exists to remove — an attacker's bundle would be re-signed
along with everyone else's unless someone curated the list, and a curation step
nobody performs is the audit that does not happen. Repair is **on demand, per
bundle, with a named reason**, and the reason belongs in the release record.

### 3. The window: 35 days, and why that number

The window is simultaneously two things, and they pull the same way:

- **the attacker's usable time after stealing the signer** — at worst the full
  window, at best whatever remains of it;
- **the release cadence being committed to** — a signer that expires with no
  reissue stops all releases, because `rauc bundle`'s read-back through
  `rauc info --keyring` verifies at the current clock and fails.

**35 days**, for three reasons that are not interchangeable:

1. It is comfortably longer than any plausible clock drift, so §5's
   *clock-ahead* brick is not reachable by ordinary error — only by a grossly
   wrong RTC.
2. RAUC's own warning fires at **under one month** (F7, measured). 35 days
   gives roughly five days of warning runway *before* expiry rather than a
   warning that arrives already late. A shorter window puts the release inside
   the warning band permanently, which trains readers to ignore it — the same
   failure `gen-dev-keys.sh` avoids by printing its notice only on the run that
   generates.
3. It is short enough that "out-waiting" a compromise is a support event
   measured in weeks rather than the two **years** the current `-days 730`
   commits to.

**Is this one operational commitment with PLAN-071 §9.6's manifest re-sign
cadence, or two?** §9.6 requires the update manifest to be re-signed before its
expiry or fielded devices report stale checks. Same shape, and the temptation
to fuse them is real. **Recommendation: one calendar, two commitments.** They
must not be one number:

- Different keys in different domains — X.509 under a CA versus lode's raw
  ed25519 (§6) — with different ceremonies and different custody.
- Different failures. A missed signer reissue stops *publishing*; a missed
  manifest re-sign makes *fielded devices* report stale. The first is visible
  to the release owner immediately; the second is visible only in the field.
- Fusing them couples the image trust domain to the package one, and
  PLAN-070 §6.2 keeps those independent at install time on purpose.

Putting both on one calendar with one owner (the release owner,
`security-lifecycle.md` §0) buys the operational convenience without buying the
coupling.

#### 3a. DECIDED — 2026-09-04: the window is **45 days**, not 35

Under §4a's decision (a), the constraint on the window stopped being RAUC's
warning band and became a **human ceremony calendar**: a signer minted at a
monthly root ceremony, where a ceremony that slips a week must not stop
releases. The user set the window at **45 days**.

§3's three reasons were re-checked rather than assumed to carry over. All three
survive, and one strengthens:

1. **Clock drift** — unchanged in kind and better in degree. 45 days puts §5's
   *clock-ahead* brick further out of reach of ordinary error.
2. **Warning runway** — this is the one that improves. 35 days gave roughly five
   days between RAUC's under-one-month warning and expiry; 45 gives roughly
   fifteen, which is the difference between a warning that can be acted on
   inside a monthly cadence and one that arrives with no time to convene a root
   ceremony. **But see the caveat below: that warning does not currently exist.**
3. **Out-waiting a compromise** — still weeks, not years. 45 days against the
   current `-days 730` is a factor of sixteen, where 35 was twenty-one. The
   attacker's usable time grows by ten days, and that is the whole price.

**Ceremony slack, which is why the number moved.** 45 days against a monthly
ceremony leaves about a fortnight of slack instead of about five days. Eleven
ceremonies a year is unchanged. A slipped ceremony becomes a scheduling problem
rather than a publishing outage.

**The caveat reason 2 rests on, and it is measured.** §M0 established that
`rauc bundle`'s *"will expire in less than a month!"* **never fires in this
tree** — `build/src/tools/rauc.ts`'s `bundleArgs` passes no `--keyring`, and
`rauc info --keyring` does not emit it either. So reason 2 describes runway
before a warning nobody currently receives. The fifteen days are real; the
warning is not, until backlog S2/S3 land. Under decision (a) those items are
already load-bearing (§4a), and this makes them more so: **45 days of runway
before a warning that is never printed is 45 days of nothing.**

Nothing else in §3 changes. The "one calendar, two commitments" conclusion about
PLAN-071 §9.6 is unaffected — the reasons it gives are about different keys,
different domains and different failure visibility, none of which turn on the
number.

### 4. Who reissues, and how often the CA key leaves its box

**The tension, stated rather than resolved by assertion.** The value of an
offline CA is that it is offline. `release-signing.md` §2.1 sizes the current
custody around it: the CA key "is needed once per signer reissue, roughly every
two years". A 35-day signer turns that into **ten to eleven ceremonies a
year**, each one a sealed-media checkout under the §1.5 custody record. That is
not a marginal change; it is a different operational posture, and it is the
real price of this plan.

Three ways out, and they are genuinely different:

- **(a) Accept monthly root access.** No PKI change. Cost: the root key is
  handled ~11×/year instead of ~0.5×, and every handling is an exposure and a
  custody-record entry. The more often a ceremony runs the more likely it is to
  be run casually.
- **(b) An intermediate CA.** Root offline and touched roughly yearly to issue
  an intermediate; the intermediate held by the release owner (a hardware token
  is the right home for it) mints 35-day signers on the monthly cadence. Root
  exposure drops back to roughly today's.
- **(c) A longer window** — 90 days, ~4 ceremonies a year — trading the
  attacker's usable time for ceremony frequency.

**Recommendation: (b), and it must be decided at the CA ceremony or not at
all.** F10 is why. `basicConstraints=critical,CA:TRUE,pathlen:0` in the root
**forbids an intermediate**, and that constraint lives in the CA certificate,
which is the keyring. Adopting (b) after a production CA exists therefore means
issuing a new root certificate and running `release-signing.md` §2.4's rollover
— which is precisely the procedure this plan does not solve and which strands
any device that misses the overlap window (§7).

The window to take this decision is open **now and only now**: PLAN-077 §4.2
records that production material has never existed, so no CA has been minted
under the constraint yet. Taking it costs one digit in an unperformed ceremony.
Declining to take it costs a fleet rollover later, or permanently commits to
(a) or (c).

What (b) additionally costs, so it is not sold cheap:

- The ceremony grows a third key with its own validity horizon and custody
  rules — an intermediate whose compromise is as bad as the root's for
  *issuing*, though not for *anchoring*, since the keyring still holds only the
  root.
- `--intermediate` must be passed at bundle time (F11): the intermediate travels
  in the bundle, not only in the issuer's safe. `build/src/tools/rauc.ts`'s
  `bundleArgs` grows a flag, and `meta/rauc/` grows a file.
- `pkgs/rauc/gen-dev-keys.sh` should mint the same shape, or the development
  chain stops being "the tested shape" the ceremony mirrors step for step —
  which is the property §2.1 explicitly relies on.

**If the user declines (b), the fallback is (c) at 90 days**, and this plan
says so rather than leaving the choice unlanded: 90 days still reduces the
attacker's window by a factor of eight against today's 730 and keeps the
ceremony count at four a year. It is a worse security answer and a defensible
operational one.

#### 4a. DECIDED — 2026-09-04: **(a), monthly root access**

The user chose **(a)**. The root CA issues each 35-day signer directly; no
intermediate is introduced, and `basicConstraints` stays `pathlen:0`.

This plan recommended (b) and named (c) as the fallback; the decision is (a).
Recorded with its consequences rather than re-argued.

**What it removes.** The time-windowed decision disappears: §P's finding that
`pathlen:0` forbids an intermediate, and that changing it after a production CA
exists means a §2.4 fleet rollover, no longer gates the first ceremony. Nothing
about the PKI shape has to be settled before production material is generated,
and the ceremony keeps mirroring the tested `gen-dev-keys.sh` chain step for
step — the property §2.1 relies on.

**What it costs, as this section already priced it.** The root key is handled
~11×/year instead of ~0.5×. Every handling is an exposure and a custody-record
entry, and the risk this section named stands unchanged: *the more often a
ceremony runs the more likely it is to be run casually.*

**Two obligations that follow, and neither existed under (b).**

1. **The ceremony becomes recurring, and PLAN-077 §7's runbook was written for a
   one-time event.** Under (b) the monthly act is minting a signer from an
   intermediate the release owner holds; under (a) it is a root ceremony —
   minutes, witness, sealed medium in and out — eleven times a year. §7 needs a
   recurring-ceremony section that is short enough to be followed monthly
   without erosion, and it must say what a monthly run may skip and what it may
   never skip. Owed to PLAN-077, not to this record.

2. **The expiry warning becomes load-bearing, and it does not exist.** §M0
   measured that `rauc bundle`'s *"will expire in less than a month!"* never
   fires in this tree, because `build/src/tools/rauc.ts`'s `bundleArgs` passes
   no `--keyring`, and that `rauc info --keyring` does not emit it either. Under
   (b) a missed mint is cheap to repair; under (a) it needs a root ceremony
   scheduled at short notice, so the fleet's releases stop until one can be
   convened. Backlog items S2/S3 are therefore not optional hardening under this
   decision — they are what stops a silent calendar slip from becoming an outage.

**One parameter left open by this choice.** §3 set 35 days partly on ~5 days of
runway before RAUC's under-a-month warning, on the assumption that a signer
could be minted on demand from an intermediate. Under (a) the constraint is a
human ceremony calendar: a 35-day certificate against a monthly ceremony leaves
about five days of slack, and a ceremony that slips a week stops releases. The
window may need to widen — 45 days keeps eleven ceremonies a year with a
fortnight of slack, and costs ten days of attacker-usable time against the 35
this plan chose. **Decided in §3a**: the window is 45 days, settled before the first
production signer is issued rather than after a slipped ceremony discovers it.

### 5. The clock — what is compared against what, and both directions of failure

**What the device compares against.** RAUC compares the certificate's validity
against **the system clock of the process performing the verification**
(measured: `faketime` alone changes the verdict, M2 vs M1). There is no way to
hand RAUC a different notion of now, so the design cannot express "compare
against the floor" directly. What it can do is guarantee the floor is *in
place* before RAUC runs — and it already is:

`docs/design/time.md` §3 establishes `max(RTC, saved clock)` before anything
that validates expiry, ordered by `mos-seed-state` →
`var-lib-systemd-timesync.mount` → `systemd-timesyncd` inside `sysinit.target`,
and everything that validates expiries starts after `sysinit.target`. RAUC's
service and any operator-invoked `rauc install` are in that set. So the bound
this plan relies on is exactly PLAN-071 §9.6's bound, and this record adopts it
rather than building a second one — **the raw RTC is never what is compared
against; the floor is the lower bound on whatever is.**

**Clock behind — the device accepts a signer that has really expired.** This is
the failure this design exists to prevent, and it is not fully prevented. It is
*bounded*, in two ways worth separating:

- The floor never sits below the last minute the device was known to be
  running: timesyncd touches its clock file every 60 s (`SaveIntervalSec`,
  pinned) and advances a clock behind that file's mtime at startup, on STATE,
  which survives reboots and A/B updates. A device that ran yesterday cannot be
  more than a boot behind.
- A device that has been **off for a year** has a floor a year old, and will
  accept signers that expired up to a year ago. Short-lived signers bound the
  attacker's window only for devices whose clocks are roughly current. For the
  ordinary update path this is largely closed by ordering — that device has no
  network, and the moment it has one, timesyncd disciplines the clock inside
  `sysinit.target`, before the update path runs. **It is not closed for a local
  `rauc install` from removable media on a device that never reaches a
  server**, and that is the residual hole. It is stated here rather than
  papered over, and it is smaller than the hole that exists today, which is
  every device for two years.

**Clock ahead — the device refuses a valid signer and cannot update at all.**
Worse than the attack, and the brief is right to call it a brick. Two things
make it worse than a transient:

- `max(RTC, saved clock)` is a **maximum**. A single grossly-wrong RTC read
  (a device with no backup power reading garbage, or a cell that failed high)
  sets the clock forward, timesyncd saves that value within 60 s, and the floor
  **preserves it across reboots** even after the RTC is corrected. NTP does
  step backwards and repairs it — but only once the device reaches a server,
  and the update path is exactly what a stranded device may be trying to use.
- The refusal is indistinguishable, in the message the operator sees, from a
  genuine trust failure: `signature verification failed: Verify error:
  certificate has expired` says nothing about the clock.

**The mitigation this plan proposes is diagnostic, not cryptographic**, because
there is no cryptographic answer to "the device's clock is wrong": the update
path must report the verification failure **together with the device's own
clock and its time status** — `GET /api/v1/time/status`'s state and the
`synchronized` bit (RFCT-299, RFCT-300). An operator who sees `certificate has
expired` next to `status: offline-degraded, clock reads 2075-01-01` diagnoses
this in seconds; one who sees the first line alone files a bug against the
release. This turns a brick into a support call, and it is the cheapest item in
§9.

Choosing 35 days rather than, say, 3 is part of the same mitigation: the window
must be far larger than any drift a working clock accumulates, so that reaching
this state requires a *broken* clock rather than a slow one.

**The warehouse year, with no RTC backup power.** cx3576 declares an
AT8563/HYM8563 RTC and its backup-power validation is an open bench question
(`time.md` §3, RFCT-280); this design assumes nothing. With **no** working RTC
the device boots to the saved floor — the last minute it ran, which for a
factory-fresh unit is the last minute of manufacturing. After a year in a box
it believes it is a year ago. The consequences, stated in the right direction:

- It does **not** brick. A clock in the past never produces `certificate has
  expired`; it produces `certificate is not yet valid` only for a signer minted
  *after* the device's belief, which for an archived bundle it already holds is
  not the case.
- It fails **open**, not closed: it will accept signers that have since
  expired, until it gets a real clock. That is the availability-safe direction
  and the revocation-unsafe one, and it is the clock-behind hole above.
- It reaches a usable clock the first time it has a network, before the update
  path runs, by `time.md` §3's ordering. **No new mechanism is required and
  none is proposed** — the warehouse case is already handled by the floor that
  RFCT-299/300 have just been through, and this plan's contribution is to say
  so explicitly rather than to add a second time source.

### 6. The package side has no PKI, and inherits nothing from this

PLAN-070 defines two signing domains: RAUC/X.509 for the image, and lode's raw
ed25519 `root.key` for the package, whose public half is baked into
`meta/updates/manifest.json`'s `trust.signingKeys`.

**Raw ed25519 keys have no chain, no validity period and no revocation list.**
There is nothing in that format for a short-lived certificate to be short-lived
*in*. So, plainly: **this plan applies to the image side. The package side's
answer to signer compromise is not improved by it, and does not inherit one.**

`trust.signingKeys` being a **list** is the nearest analogue, and the brief is
right that it is only an analogue: adding a key is an overlap window and
removing one revokes — but removing an entry needs a **new baked manifest**,
which needs a new image, which is the anchor problem again (PLAN-070 §6.3,
which records that the package anchor inherits both of `release-signing.md`
§2.3's uncovered cases verbatim). A compromised package key is out-waited by an
image update, not by a clock, and no device-side check shortens that.

What the package side *does* have is PLAN-071 §9.6's freshness bound on the
manifest — and it is important not to over-read it. **It bounds metadata
staleness, not key compromise.** An attacker holding `root.key` signs fresh
manifests indefinitely; every freshness check passes. §9.6 closes the freeze
attack, not the theft.

Making the package side comparable would mean giving it a certificate format
and a chain, i.e. changing lode's key format — explicitly out of scope
(§8).

### 7. What this does **not** close

Stated as plainly as PLAN-077 §4 states its own limits.

- **CA compromise is untouched.** Neither an expiry nor a CRL helps when the
  thing being replaced is what signs them: a short signer is issued by the CA,
  and a CRL is signed by the CA. An attacker holding the CA key mints their own
  fresh signer whenever they like, and this plan does not slow them by one day.
- **CA rotation is untouched.** `release-signing.md` §2.4's overlap procedure,
  its missed-window case and its compromise caveat all stand exactly as
  written. §4's recommendation (b) *interacts* with this — choosing `pathlen:1`
  late means performing that rotation — but the plan adds no capability to it.
- **The CRL question is answered, not opened.** rauc 1.13 does support
  `[keyring] check-crl` (F12), so the mechanism exists on the device. What does
  not exist is delivery, and a CRL has the *same* clock dependency as an expiry
  plus a distribution channel this device does not have — and a stale CRL
  forces the fail-open/fail-closed choice the brief describes. Short validity
  is preferred here precisely because it needs no channel. `check-crl` stays
  unset.
- **PLAN-077 §6's second-anchor question shrinks rather than disappears.** With
  signer compromise answered by expiry, the question §6.5 puts to the user
  narrows from "do we need a device-time trust-rotation channel" to **"is a
  third offline key worth it for CA compromise alone"** — a rarer event, a
  narrower justification, and therefore an easier thing to defer. That is a
  change in the *shape* of the question, not an answer to it; §6 remains the
  record that owns it and this plan does not restate its design.

### 8. What it costs to give up — nothing, and that is the argument

PLAN-070 §6.3's immutability property is the reason to prefer this route:

> there is nothing to distribute in the ordinary case and nothing on the device
> that can be rewritten to change what it trusts.

**Expiry trades none of it.** No anchor changes. `meta/rauc/ca.cert.pem` stays
the single source by which a trust root enters a build; `rootfs/build.sh`'s
refusal of the overlay is untouched; `packed-keyring-from-meta`'s byte-equality
assertion is untouched; nothing on the device becomes writable; no new key is
baked; `META_PUBLIC` does not grow a member. The device gains the ability to
stop trusting a stolen signer **without receiving anything**, which is the one
capability an offline device can actually have.

Contrast the second anchor (PLAN-077 §6): it buys more — rotation away from a
compromised **CA** — and it pays with exactly that property, introducing a
STATE-backed keyring behind a bind mount and a device-side writer. This plan is
the cheaper half of the same problem, and it is cheap because it changes no
mechanism at all (§1). **That is the reason to take this route first and decide
§6.5 later, on a narrower question.**

## Risks

- **The cadence is a standing operational commitment, and the failure mode is
  a stopped release line.** A missed reissue does not degrade gracefully: the
  bundle build's read-back fails and nothing ships. Mitigation is §9's
  pre-flight gate (F7's warning, made visible and then fatal at a threshold),
  plus the calendar of §3. Residual risk: an organisation that cannot keep a
  monthly ceremony should choose §4(c), and should choose it deliberately
  rather than by missing dates.
- **One config line disables the whole design (F4).** `use-bundle-signing-time=true`
  in a rendered `system.conf` would make every measurement above read
  `ACCEPTED`, including the forgery. §1's asserted-absence check is the
  mitigation and it is the highest-value item in §9.
- **The same line is *required*, locally, on the release host (F9).** A
  procedure that tells an operator to set it is one keystroke from a device
  config. The runbook must make the two files visibly different objects, and
  the release host's config must never be a copy of the device's.
- **Clock-ahead remains a real brick path** and this plan mitigates it
  diagnostically, not preventively (§5). A device with a grossly wrong RTC and
  no network cannot update. This is not new — TLS and §9.6's freshness check
  have the same dependency — but shortening the signer makes the window smaller
  and so makes the state marginally easier to reach.
- **Clock-behind means revocation is weakest exactly where the device is most
  isolated** (§5). Honest bound: this design shortens the attacker's window for
  connected devices and does much less for a device that never reaches a
  server.
- **The `pathlen` decision expires.** §4(b) is free today and costs a fleet
  rollover after the first production CA ceremony. If this plan is approved and
  the ceremony is performed before the question is answered, option (b) is
  gone.
- **Re-signing changes release bytes** (§2), so the archive-repair path touches
  `SHA256SUMS` and `manifest.json`. Designing that record is deferred to the
  backlog; the risk is that it is discovered during an incident rather than
  before one.

## Scope

**In:** the device's verification semantics and the assertion that they are
deliberate; the signer validity window and its operational commitment; the
reissue authority and the intermediate-CA decision, with its measured
constraint; the archive-repair procedure; the clock's role in both failure
directions; the package side's non-inheritance; the ceremony and runbook text
these imply.

**Out:** any implementation. CA rotation and the §2.4 rollover. PLAN-077 §6's
second anchor. Changes to lode's key format or the package trust model. A
control-plane-delivered revocation service. Enabling `check-crl`.
HSM/PKCS#11 wiring for the signer key (`release-signing.md` §2.1 records its
absence; this plan does not close it, though §4(b)'s token would touch it).

## Alternatives

- **Keep `-days 730` and out-wait a compromise.** The status quo. Costs
  nothing, and prices a stolen signer at two years of fleet-wide exposure.
  Rejected because that is the gap the tree already records as a gap.
- **A CRL delivered to devices.** Mechanically supported (F12). Rejected for
  the brief's own reason, which the measurement supports: it needs a channel
  the offline device does not have, and a stale CRL forces a choice between
  refusing all updates and accepting a revoked key. Expiry replaces "did a
  revocation reach me" with "what time is it", which a device can answer alone.
- **`use-bundle-signing-time=true` on the device, keeping bundles installable
  forever.** Rejected on measurement, not on taste: F4 shows it accepts a
  bundle signed by a signer that expired 370 days ago. It is not a weaker
  revocation, it is none.
- **A very short window (days).** Rejected: it puts every release permanently
  inside RAUC's warning band (F7), makes §5's clock-ahead brick reachable by
  ordinary drift, and multiplies the ceremony count without shrinking the
  attacker's window in any way that matters — an attacker who steals a signer
  uses it in hours, not weeks.
- **Patch RAUC to verify against a trusted timestamp.** There is no RFC 3161
  support to build on (F12), it would fork the pinned upstream that
  `versions.env` exists to avoid forking, and a timestamp authority is another
  service the offline device cannot reach. Rejected.
- **§4(a) monthly root access, and §4(c) a 90-day window.** Both live
  alternatives to (b) rather than rejected ones; §4 recommends (b) and says
  what each costs.

## Approval boundary

**This plan ends here and authorises nothing.** What approval would mean,
exactly:

- the device verifies signer validity against **now**, which is what RAUC 1.13
  does by default and what the tree already ships — **no mechanism change** —
  and the absence of `use-bundle-signing-time` becomes an **asserted** property
  of the rendered `system.conf` rather than an incidental one (§1);
- the signer validity window becomes **35 days**, with §3's three reasons, and
  it is an **operational commitment to a release cadence** as much as a
  security parameter;
- that commitment shares a calendar with PLAN-071 §9.6's manifest re-sign
  cadence but is **a separate commitment**, for §3's three reasons;
- an archived bundle whose signer expired is repaired by **`rauc resign` on the
  release host** under a signer reissued from the unchanged CA, with the
  release host — and only the release host — running
  `use-bundle-signing-time=true` locally (§2, measured F8/F9); a resigned bundle
  is a **new release record**, not an in-place edit;
- a **factory reflash is unaffected**, because it writes the `.img` and not the
  `.raucb` (Context);
- the reissue authority is the release owner, and the recommended posture is an
  **intermediate CA** so the root leaves its box roughly yearly rather than
  monthly — **which requires `pathlen:1` in the root certificate and must
  therefore be decided at the CA ceremony or not at all** (§4, measured F10);
  §4(c)'s 90-day window is the named fallback if (b) is declined;
- the clock this rests on is `docs/design/time.md` §3's
  `max(RTC, saved clock)` floor, **adopted rather than rebuilt**, with the
  clock-behind hole and the clock-ahead brick stated in §5 and the latter
  mitigated **diagnostically** by reporting the verification failure beside the
  device's time status;
- the **package side gets nothing from this plan and inherits nothing** (§6);
  `trust.signingKeys` being a list is an analogue, and removing an entry is the
  anchor problem again;
- **CA compromise and CA rotation are untouched** (§7), and PLAN-077 §6's
  second-anchor question **shrinks** to "is a third offline key worth it for CA
  compromise alone" rather than disappearing — §6.5 remains where that decision
  lives;
- **PLAN-070 §6.3's immutability property is not traded** (§8), which is the
  reason to prefer this route over the second anchor.

**The decisions the user must take**, both in §4 and both genuinely open:

1. **(b), (a) or (c)** — intermediate CA, monthly root access, or a 90-day
   window. This plan recommends (b) and names (c) as the fallback.
2. If (b): **the `pathlen:1` change to the CA ceremony must be taken before the
   first production ceremony runs.** After that it is a fleet rollover.

Approval does **not** authorise performing any ceremony, editing
`docs/design/release-signing.md`, changing `pkgs/rauc/gen-dev-keys.sh`, or
writing any of §9.
